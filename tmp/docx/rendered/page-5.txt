Reviewer#1, Concern#7: The RDODA algorithm retains delay-violating solutions by
assigning negative fitness values in the delay direction, instead of removing them. The
manuscript does not explain how negative values affect non-dominated sorting.

Author response:
Thanks for the suggestion. The revised DAREED algorithm no longer relies on an
unexplained negative-fitness treatment. Instead, it uses a constraint-aware evaluation
mechanism. Delay violation, CPU violation, and path feasibility violation are
accumulated into a constraint violation value. Feasible individuals are preferred over
infeasible individuals; among infeasible individuals, those with smaller violations are
preferred. This avoids distorting Pareto dominance while still preserving useful search
information in early generations.
