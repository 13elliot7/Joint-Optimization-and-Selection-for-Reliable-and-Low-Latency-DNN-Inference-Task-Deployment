Response to Reviewer #1

Reviewer#1, Concern#1: In the Abstract, the manuscript claims to use NSGA-II “to
optimize both reliability and delay”, but it then explicitly lists service reliability,
accuracy reliability, and delay as optimization objectives, which is effectively a three-
objective formulation. Without clarifying that “reliability” is decomposed into service
and accuracy aspects, this wording may appear internally inconsistent and weaken the
clarity of the main contribution.

Author response:
Thanks for pointing out this inconsistency. We have revised the terminology
throughout the manuscript. The term “accuracy reliability” has been replaced by
inference accuracy, and the optimization objectives are now consistently stated as
service reliability, inference accuracy, inference delay, and total energy
consumption. This avoids mixing service availability and inference quality under an
ambiguous reliability term.

Author action:
We revised the Abstract, Introduction, Problem Formulation, Algorithm Design, and
Performance Analysis sections to use the four objective terms consistently.

Reviewer#1, Concern#2: In Related Work, the manuscript uses strongly judgmental
phrasing such as “relying on fixed, hand-designed reward weights is outdated” and
suggests that preference-driven systems can “select the perfect deployment strategy
instantly”. This tone reads as subjective and promotional.

Author response:
Thanks for the suggestion. We agree that the previous wording was overly
promotional. The revised Related Work section now uses neutral, evidence-based
language. It discusses the limitations of fixed weighted aggregation in dynamic DNN
inference deployment without using subjective expressions.

Author action:
We rewrote the Related Work discussion on multi-objective optimization and
preference-based decision making. The revised text emphasizes that fixed weights are
difficult to determine under heterogeneous request preferences and time-varying
system states.
